Six weeks or eight

The 22 open challenges from the three release meetings on Sep 2 and Sep 3, grouped by where they bite. Numbered so they can be called out by number in the room.

Context: the three models these challenges attach to

A. Eight weeks external, six internal
Agreed on the Sep 3 morning call. Five weeks dev and QA, week 6 regression, two weeks of client UAT, branch cut 9/25, UAT 10/11, production 10/25.
B. Six weeks, internal and external in sync
Agreed on the Sep 3 afternoon call. Four weeks planned work, week 5 buffer, week 6 regression, branch cut 9/18, UAT 9/25 or 9/28, production 10/11 unchanged.
C. One release behind
Raised by Prashant on the Fairfax model, opposed by Seshagiri and Zeke. Skip a public release and let the code sit in UAT for a full cycle.

Cadence and calendar 3

01
The two Sep 3 meetings ended on different cadencesDisputed

The morning call landed on model A and that outcome was headed to Brian and Harpy. The afternoon call landed on model B. Prashant and Zeke were absent for most of the afternoon, so A has never been formally retired and is still what leadership expects. Inference from the notes, not stated on either call.

02
Internal and external cadences cannot run at different lengthsDisputed

They drift apart every cycle. Sid: a 6-week internal against an 8-week external "cannot work, it has to be in sync." Prashant raised the same risk himself, that the team could end up working on one release while shipping one two releases old, then still proposed the split.

03
Three sets of dates are in circulation, and two of them disagree with themselvesOpen

The afternoon notes say 9/25 in the detail and 9/28 in the summary; the morning notes say both 10/23 and 10/25 for production. Eight weeks also means four sprints per release, so it changes the internal process, not just the calendar. Seshagiri: "without changing calendar dates our current process will work."

Hotfixes while a release is held 4

04
How long release code sits unreleased is the core objectionDisputed

Four weeks under model A, roughly six under model C, and hotfixes have to be maintained against a held branch the whole time. Seshagiri expects critical hotfixes for the next couple of releases regardless, and this is his stated single reason for opposing C. Prashant: a shorter interval means fewer hotfixes, and every approach has costs.

05
No branching or merge strategy exists for a UAT hotfixOpen

Fix on DevOps, push to UAT, then merge back into staging and the release build. Acknowledged as "a little complicated" and left there.

06
Critical bug on Friday, UAT deploy on SundayOpen

Does it get fixed on staging or on UAT, and who tests it where? Suvarna asked directly. No answer was given on the call.

07
The freeze exception path has never been usedAgreed

Nothing goes in during the final week unless three or four leads agree the use case on a call, and non-critical post-release hotfixes hold until the next morning. Both rules are agreed and untested, and enforcement sits with Prashant, Seshagiri and Sid.

Whether one week of regression is real 5

08
QA says one week is hardly possible, Prashant says it isDisputed

Suvarna: closing development in week 5 and finishing regression in the same window needs very strict planning and is "hardly possible." Prashant: RC1 is pretty darn comprehensive and gets done in 48 hours, so a focused week should do it.

09
Spillover out of week 5 is the failure modeDisputed

Zeke called spillover guaranteed. Seshagiri says planning has to eliminate it. Model B answers this by planning only four weeks and holding week 5 as buffer, which cuts planned capacity per release and needs product and leadership to accept that.

10
No rule for which regression bugs get fixed in the final weekOpen

Sid's position: fix only what this release's work broke, defer anything already live in production. Agreed in conversation, never written down as the rule.

11
RC1 has to run before the UAT pushOpen

The build going to UAT must be release-ready, so either RC1 runs twice per release or another environment is needed. Prashant raised it and set it aside as an environment question.

12
QA workload is not balanced across the cycleOpen

Development closing Wednesday to give QA Thursday and Friday is the target and it slips to Saturday. QA also has to close release work in week 5 and pick up UAT support in weeks 5 and 6. Suvarna: this needs planning very carefully because it is the first time.

Environments 4

13
DevOps or staging for the pre-release buildDisputed

Formally still open from the Sep 2 call. Seshagiri wants DevOps because nobody deploys manually there and few people have access. Prashant questions the logistics and sees staging as the natural home, frozen and mirroring production.

14
Whichever environment is chosen, it has to be cleared and loadedOpen

No manual DLL, assembly, JS or script changes in the final week, which displaces the database restores and breaking-change testing that use staging in that window. Client verification also only means something on restored production data for the large accounts. Neither has been scoped.

15
The caveat list Harpy asked for does not exist yetOpen

What will not work in the client UAT environment: communicator app, interfaces, billing, SSO, Form.IO needing separate tenants, non-production data refresh. It was the first action item out of the Sep 3 morning call.

16
Containers or on-prem, and who owns the environment long termOpen

Deferred to a technical session with Venu, Andrew, Civa and Gopi that has not happened. Permanent client access with a fresh build every cycle also carries an ongoing support load nobody has costed.

Discipline, and the failure that started this 3

17
The root cause was manual deployment, not cadenceAgreed

A batch file pointed at an old hotfix folder and a database component outside the pull request went to production during a rushed late-night fix. No cadence change addresses that. Environment control and the freeze do.

18
QA and the deployment team still have not been in the roomOpen

Deployment is where the failure actually happened. Prashant flagged this twice on Sep 2 and it is still an open action.

19
"Treat UAT as production" is load-bearing and unenforcedOpen

The whole model collapses if the team treats the UAT build as lower grade. Prashant: any room to slip will get taken, "I have two weeks to fix it." Sid: without this, doing all of it adds no value. A customer finding a bug in UAT should escalate like a red-ball event, which is undefined.

People and stakeholders 3

20
The capacity question is coming, whichever model winsOpen

Two hardening weeks per cycle invites "we are spending more on hardening than on new features." Prashant and Zeke both anticipated it. Four weeks of planned work in a six-week cycle raises the same question in a different form.

21
One release is not an evidence base, and teams have just been rewired onceOpen

Zeke: nothing yet shows the hardening sprint succeeding or failing, and the deployment failure was independent of it. Prashant: too much change and people get confused and make mistakes. Sid: it deserves another release or two before being judged.

22
The fixes depend on people outside this roomOpen

Requirement clarity on day one and splitting work items small enough for QA to start early is the upstream cause of spillover. Capacity only returns if product is ready for the next release, which needs Brian. And staying out of panic mode on the largest account has to hold team-wide, not just between Sid and Seshagiri.

What has to be decided today

  1. One cadence, one calendar. Settles 01, 02, 03. The reframe that helps: the client asked for a UAT environment, not for a particular cadence.
  2. The hotfix path for a held release. Settles 04, 05, 06. Otherwise this reopens the cadence decision later.
  3. Owners for 15, 16, 13 and 18. The caveat list, the environment session, DevOps versus staging, and bringing QA and deployment in.
  4. What leadership is told, and by whom. Brian and Harpy currently expect model A.
