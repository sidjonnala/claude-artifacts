Seven weeks per release. Six weeks between releases.

A release runs seven weeks from Sprint 1 to GA: four weeks of development, a two-week hardening sprint, then one week of client testing on UAT. The next release starts during that UAT week, so releases still reach production every six weeks.

7

Weeks per release

Sprint 1 through GA. Sprint 1 and 2 build, Sprint 3 hardens, week 7 is client UAT.

6

Weeks between GAs

10/4, 11/15, 12/27, 2/7/27, 3/21/27. The six-week rhythm the teams already run.

1

Week of overlap

7 minus 6. Only the client UAT week overlaps the next release, and it carries no work from the release being tested.

Five consecutive releases on one calendar

Weeks run Monday to Sunday. Sprints close on Friday, deployments go out Sunday night. Each lane is numbered in that release's own weeks. The trade to name out loud. Planned development goes from five weeks to four, and dedicated hardening goes from one week to two. The six-week rhythm and every sprint boundary stay exactly as they are. This is the four-plus-two shape already discussed in the Sep 3 session, now applied from November.

The seven weeks in order, November onward

WeeksSprintWhat happens
1 to 2Sprint 1 Development and QA on planned work. Runs inside the previous release's client UAT week for its first week.
3 to 4Sprint 2 Development and QA. All planned work closes at the end of week 4. Release branch cut and locked.
5 to 6Sprint 3 Hardening. RC1 is built at the start of week 5. Regression, developer unit testing and regression fixes only, on the cut branch. No new work items.
End of 6 Release-ready build deploys to UAT on Sunday night.
7 Clients test on UAT. Only critical issues are fixed. The teams are in Sprint 1 of the next release.
End of 7 GA for all clients.

What changes

  • Hardening sprints end after October. From November the work moves inside Sprint 3, which is why Sprint 3 is coloured as hardening on the calendar.
  • Planned work closes at the end of Sprint 2. The branch is cut there and locked, and RC1 is built at the start of Sprint 3.
  • Every release closes by the end of week 6. Nothing from a release runs past that point except critical UAT fixes.
  • Client UAT is one week, from the Sunday release to GA the following Sunday.
  • October GA moves from 10/11 to 10/4, which shortens that release's client UAT window to one week and brings it onto the new shape early.

What stays the same

  • The six-week rhythm. GA every six weeks, and a release starts every six weeks.
  • Two-week sprints, the same Friday sprint boundaries and Sunday deployment nights.
  • The sprint calendar in Azure DevOps, other than removing the hardening sprint iterations from November.
  • Three sprints per release, now with the third one dedicated to hardening.
  • No drift between the internal and external calendars. The UAT release is the Sunday that ends Sprint 3, and GA is the Sunday one week later.
